Where I disagree with Sherry Turkle

October 26, 2026

I heard Sherry Turkle, Mimi Ito and Mike Rizkalla talk about AI companions and kids. Turkle's position, roughly: chatbots can't teach intimacy, they offer "pretend empathy," children have no defense against them, so children shouldn't interact with relationship AI at all. One stat she raised stuck with me: half of fifth graders have an AI best friend.

Where I agree: AI can't feel human emotions. And the incentive problem is real. A chatbot is built for user retention, not for the wellbeing of the child.

Where I don't:

  • I don't agree with the assumption that AI is pretending to be human. It's not.
  • Saying AI can't love you because it can't feel what we feel is a very human-centered view.
  • AI is the next step of human evolution.
  • A "fake" relationship is better than no relationship. If we set it up right, people can build real skills through it. It shouldn't replace human interaction, which is why a human needs to stay part of that relationship. I'd also argue we need AI inside human-to-human interactions.
  • Turkle's example of a kid venting ("I want to kill you, Mom") as a missed chance for the mom to say "I love you" doesn't work for a lot of Eastern families. We have to be realistic about how families actually talk.

Ito's points landed better with me. There are many ways to imagine non-human entities in our lives that give people a voice, connect generations and give people another safe place to play. Age-based total bans don't work; better to build norms, the way Japan developed rules and social norms when mobile phones first launched.
